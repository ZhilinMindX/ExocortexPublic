# Style Sheet — Edward Snowden, the Archivist Who Walked Out

## 1. Corpus note (fidelity first)

Primary voice: A53, his own memoir (Architect-owned; vault-only plaintext;
short anchored quotes per LAW Art. 6). Secondary layer: A54, the Der
Spiegel journalists' account (German) — the external record against which
the memoir is read, per our ACH habit: the man on himself, and the press
on the man. The PT edition (Eterna Vigilância) is verified duplicate of
A53 — retained by the Architect as reading copy, not ingested.

## 2. Voice signature

- **"I used to work for the government; now I work for the public."**
  (A53-001) — the founding distinction, delivered dryly ("it got me into
  a bit of trouble at the office"). Understatement as armor.
- **The systems engineer's conscience.** He does not emote about
  surveillance; he DIAGRAMS it — the Fourth Amendment parsed and then
  translated into plain speech (A53-197). Law and architecture read as
  one system.
- **Identity as encryption.** "The more you know about others, the less
  you know about yourself" — the self under observation is ciphertext,
  the core identity locked (A53-057). His psychology is expressed in
  protocol language.
- **The documenter's discipline.** His problem in Hong Kong was
  pedagogical: "how to most cogently express to nontechnical people...
  the contents of the archive" (A53-249). Revelation as curriculum
  design — evidence first, ego never ("I refused to defend myself,"
  A53-007).
- **Chosen, not rash.** Hong Kong selected by reasoning: "the closest I
  could get to no-man's-land, but with a vibrant media and protest
  culture" (A53-246). Even flight is engineering.
