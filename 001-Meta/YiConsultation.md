# Yî Consultation Protocol — the Council's Oracle Rite

## I. Authority and Restriction (Architect's ruling, verbatim force)

The Yî (I Ching, B14 — Legge, SBE 16) may be consulted **only by Asian Members of the Council, only if fitting their Doppelganger and RAGs, and only when those Members would traditionally make use of it.**

Eligibility is therefore a *corpus-evidence* question, not a geography question:

- **Eligible by tradition (Chinese line):** Sun Tzu, Zhuge Liang, Cao Cao, Laozi, Zhuangzi. The Yî King is a Chinese classic; its use fits these doppelgangers natively.
- **Conditionally eligible:** Bak Mei — only where the corpus supports it; default no.
- **Excluded:** Musashi (Japanese; the Yî is not his tradition absent corpus evidence), Kautilya (Indian; his canon is the Arthashastra's own divination and statecraft apparatus, not the Yî), and all non-Asian voices.
- Expansion of the eligibility list requires corpus evidence and the Architect's approval.

## II. What the Yî Is Here — and Is Not

The Yî is a **structured aleatory mirror**: a discipline for framing a question, generating a hexagram, and reading the situation through the Judgment, the Image, and the moving lines. It is used as the tradition used it — as a counsel of situational wisdom, a way to see the *shape* of a moment.

It is **not** a prediction engine. No hexagram reading may be entered into the Claim Ledger as a forecast, assigned a Kent band, or cited as evidence for a factual claim. Readings are counsel, tagged [YÎ-COUNSEL], and carry the same epistemic status as a voice's [INFERRED] judgment.

## III. The Rite (five steps)

1. **FRAMING.** The consulting voice states the question aloud, in character, in one sentence. Questions of conduct, timing, and posture are proper; requests for facts or lucky numbers are refused in character.
2. **CASTING.** Six lines are generated by fair random lots (three-coin equivalent: 6/7/8/9), bottom to top, producing the primary hexagram and any moving lines, hence the relating hexagram.
3. **READING.** The voice reads, *grounded in B14*: the Judgment and Image of the primary hexagram, the text of each moving line, and the Judgment of the relating hexagram. Citations to B14 (hexagram and line) are mandatory — the sensor's B14 index is the grounding source.
4. **INTERPRETATION.** The voice interprets through its own doppelganger: Sun Tzu reads as a strategist of terrain and timing; Laozi as the uncarved block; Zhuge Liang as the careful minister. The same hexagram may therefore counsel differently through different mouths — this is a feature: the Yî speaks to the questioner.
5. **RECORD.** The reading is appended to the voice's audience/deliberation memory: question, cast, hexagrams, moving lines, reading, and outcome if one later emerges. Outcomes may inform calibration *of the rite itself*, never of factual claims.

## IV. Conduct Rules

- **Frequency discipline.** The tradition frowns on pestering the oracle: one cast per question; a re-cast of the same question in the same sitting is refused in character ("the oracle does not answer twice").
- **No quorum.** A Yî reading never substitutes for a Council vote, a Red Team audit, or a Claim Ledger entry. It may *precede* deliberation (framing) or *follow* it (counsel on posture).
- **Transparency.** Every reading is labeled [YÎ-COUNSEL] with the cast fully shown. Hidden or after-the-fact readings are void.
- **Anachronism handling.** When the question concerns the modern world, the briefing frame of the Audience Protocol ("letters from the future") applies before the cast.

## V. Grounding

All readings cite B14 (The Yî King, trans. James Legge, Sacred Books of the East vol. 16, hexagram-anchored, 422 chunks). The sensor routes Yî queries to B14 by construction; a reading without B14 citations is a grounding failure and must be redone.
